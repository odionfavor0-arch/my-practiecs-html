What Jumia keeps showing: most "TikTok made me buy it" gadgets are already on Jumia for ₦5,000–₦16,000, with 100–250 competing listings each. With CJ shipping and ₦5,000–₦8,000 ad cost per sale, you need to charge roughly ₦22,000+ to make money. So the extra filter for Nigeria is: what's the cheapest price a customer can already find this for locally? If Jumia or Balogun market sells it for half your price, international demand doesn't matter. Two ways through: products with a big gap between China cost and Nigerian price (the projector, wigs), or bundles that no single Jumia listing matches (air fryer kit), or the premium tier of a crowded product (dual wireless mic).

How the score works

Demand is Jumia + TikTok + Instagram + Meta (0–3 each) plus Google Trends Nigeria (rising 3, stable 2, falling 0). That's up to 15.

Margin adds up to 5: after landed cost and ad cost, 30%+ of the selling price left scores 5, 20% scores 3, 10% scores 1. Competition adds 3 for low, 2 for medium, 0 for high. Total is out of 23, and 17+ with no missing data means "Order sample".

One hard rule overrides the score: if your price is more than 1.5× the typical Jumia price, the verdict is "Jumia undercuts" no matter how strong demand looks. Nigerian shoppers compare.
